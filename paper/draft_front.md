# Neighbours, Not Selves: How Self-Conditioning Coordinates Parallel Decoding in Flow Language Models

## Abstract

Continuous flow and diffusion language models decode several tokens in parallel, and almost all of them rely on self-conditioning, which feeds each position's previous prediction back as an input at the next denoising step. Self-conditioning is known to improve sample quality, but why it does so remains unexplained. We study Lafa, a blockwise flow language model, with causal interventions that route the fed-back predictions through attention. Without self-conditioning, parallel positions decoded independently produce inconsistent joint outputs, including cross-answer hybrids such as "San York" and duplicated tokens. A pre-registered decomposition on held-out data shows that the repair runs almost entirely through each position reading its neighbours' fed-back predictions rather than its own: the neighbour route carries 0.99 to 1.14 of the effect at 0.5B under two independent judges, and a position's own feedback contributes nothing or slightly harms coherence. Changing the sampler's noise schedule moves this effect in time, and a pre-registered test on 1,000 further documents confirms that positions learn their neighbours' content through two redundant channels, the neighbours' states and their fed-back predictions, with the feedback necessary exactly while the states are uninformative. A set of 26 attention heads carries this reading: removing their access to the feedback nearly triples cross-answer and garbled outputs at deployed settings, while matched random heads do not, and transplanting neighbours' fed-back predictions from another run creates hybrids on demand. A model trained from scratch with neighbour-only self-conditioning matches or exceeds full self-conditioning, while own-only self-conditioning is no better than none. Self-conditioning in parallel decoders is therefore best understood as a learned message channel between positions.

## 1. Introduction

Parallel decoding is the main promise of non-autoregressive language models: a block of tokens is denoised jointly over a fixed number of steps rather than generated one token at a time [Gu et al. 2018; Ghazvininejad et al. 2019]. Its main hazard is also old. When positions predict their tokens without seeing what their neighbours will commit to, the product of per-position marginals can assign high probability to combinations no single answer contains, the multimodality problem first described for non-autoregressive translation [Gu et al. 2018]. Recent continuous flow and diffusion language models reach competitive quality at scale [Chavan et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2026], and nearly all of them use self-conditioning (SC), in which each position receives its own previous prediction as an extra input [Chen et al. 2022; Dieleman et al. 2022; Strudel et al. 2022].

Why SC helps is an open question. Existing accounts treat it as a per-position or temporal mechanism: it corrects a train/inference mismatch and improves token identifiability [Davis et al. 2026], implements fixed-point refinement [Yoo et al. 2026], keeps trajectories out of low-density regions [Shabalin et al. 2026], or repeats information the solver has already folded into the latent [Li & Xu 2026]. Pynadath et al. [2025] suggest, without testing, that previous predictions may serve as anchors for other positions. These accounts make different predictions about where SC's benefit comes from, but none separates the routes by intervention.

We answer the question causally in Lafa [Chavan et al. 2026], a blockwise flow language model that denoises four tokens at a time. We first confirm on held-out data that, without SC, parallel decoding fails in both classic ways, and that SC repairs in-block coherence under the model's own judge and an external one. We then decompose SC's benefit into three routes with a 2 × 2 × 2 intervention that controls, for a target position, whether it receives its own fed-back prediction, whether it reads its neighbours' fed-back predictions through attention, and whether the neighbours' own trajectories receive feedback. Every claim that carries the argument was pre-registered, with its hash anchored in a public repository before the confirmation data existed, and every deviation was logged before the affected data.

Our contributions are as follows.

- **Neighbours, not selves.** On 2,000 held-out documents, the neighbour-reading route carries 0.99 to 1.14 of SC's coherence and dependence benefit at 0.5B (Shapley share; external judge 1.04), while a position's own feedback contributes nothing or slightly harms (Section 5). The same division appears in a model trained from scratch, where neighbour-only SC matches or exceeds full SC and own-only SC is no better than none.
- **When and why.** The effect sits in the late denoising steps under Lafa's default schedule because that schedule keeps states uninformative until a final jump. Changing the schedule moves the effect, and a pre-registered test on 1,000 further documents confirms a two-channel account in which states and fed-back predictions carry the same neighbour information redundantly (Section 6).
- **Where.** A distributed set of 26 attention heads in the middle and late layers carries the reading. Cutting their access to neighbour feedback nearly triples cross-answer and garbled outputs at deployed settings, against no effect for matched random heads (Section 7).
- **Steering by message transplant.** Replacing only the neighbours' fed-back predictions with those of a run that committed to the other answer produces hybrids on demand, and only when done in the late window (Section 7).
- **Methods.** A causal decomposition of a recurrent feedback channel, per-query key/value routing for patching through the denoising-time feedback edge, and schedule manipulation as a falsification tool for mechanistic accounts (Section 2).

Section 2 describes Lafa, self-conditioning, our measures and the interventions. Section 3 establishes the failure that SC repairs and Section 4 the repair. Sections 5 to 7 give the mechanism, its timing and its localisation. Section 8 tests generality at 7B and in a second architecture, Section 9 places the results in the literature and Section 10 discusses implications and limits.

## 2. Setting, measures and interventions

**Model and decoding.** Lafa decodes a response in blocks of four positions. Within a block every position starts from Gaussian noise in token-embedding space and is denoised over K = 32 Euler steps with bidirectional attention inside the block and causal attention to the prefix. Self-conditioning modifies the input of position i at step k to x̃_i = x_i + W₁x_i + W₂(q_i E), where x_i is the noisy state, q_i is the position's predicted distribution from step k − 1 and E is the embedding matrix. Lafa trains with SC dropped for the whole batch with probability 0.5, so SC-off decoding (q E replaced by zero) is in-distribution. The model's output is the final step's argmax prediction. We use the 0.5B checkpoint for all confirmatory tests and the 7B checkpoint for scale.

**Regimes.** Lafa is deployed with classifier-free prefix guidance g = 8 and inference noise 0.1, the paper regime (PR). We run mechanism analyses in a regime without guidance and with the training noise scale 4.0, the mechanism regime (MR), because guidance introduces a second feedback state per branch. We test each mechanistic claim in PR as well. The SC effect is equivalent across the two guidance settings and across the two noise settings (Section 4), and SC's end-task benefit and coherence gain are present in MR.

**Measures.** For position j of a generated block we define coherence, coh_j = log p(y_j | c, y_<j), under Lafa's own autoregressive head, and in-block dependence, dep_j = coh_j − log of the mean probability of y_j under 16 predecessor sequences sampled from the same head. dep_j measures how much the token depends on the specific predecessors the block produced. Because both measures use Lafa's own head, we also score every output with Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, which shares no weights with Lafa. For bimodal prompts (choose one of two named entities) we classify each answer as one of the two valid answers, a hybrid formed from a prefix of one and a suffix of the other at token boundaries, or other; the classifier agreed with a 150-label audit on 98.7 per cent of items and on all hybrids.

**The 2 × 2 × 2 decomposition.** At every denoising step we run two forward passes on the same states: pass A with SC at a chosen set of positions and pass B with SC at another. For target j, three binary factors select (O) whether j's own input carries its feedback, (N) whether j's attention reads its neighbours' keys and values from the pass in which they carry feedback, and (F) whether the neighbours' own outputs, and hence their next states and feedback, come from that pass. Per-query key/value routing is implemented by appending both passes' keys and values to the cache and masking each query's reads. The (1,1,1) and (0,0,0) cells reproduce normal SC-on and SC-off decoding token for token, and we check both identities on every block. We report Möbius main effects, Shapley shares and the pre-registered linear contrast N − 0.20·T, where T is the total SC effect.

**Localisation and steering.** Head-level cuts replace a target's reads of its neighbours' keys and values, at chosen heads, layers and steps, with those from a pass in which the neighbours receive no feedback; all compared conditions run through the same code path. For steering we record a donor run's fed-back predictions at every step and let the target read its neighbours through (recipient states, donor feedback).

**Pre-registration and analysis.** The primary family (hybrid excess, SC repair, neighbour share) was tested with Holm correction over three tests and cluster bootstrap intervals with 20,000 resamples; secondary tests used Benjamini–Hochberg control at q = 0.10. Later families (the two-channel account, the 7B mechanism, the second architecture and the head tests) were each pre-registered with their own decision rules before their data existed. Exploratory analyses are labelled as such throughout.
