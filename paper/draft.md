# Neighbours, Not Selves: How Self-Conditioning Coordinates Parallel Decoding in Flow Language Models

## 3. Parallel decoding fails in two ways without self-conditioning

We first establish the phenomenon that self-conditioning (SC) has to solve. Lafa generates each four-token block in parallel, so every position predicts its token without seeing the tokens its neighbours will finally commit to. When a prompt admits two valid answers, independent per-position choices can combine into an answer that belongs to neither, the multimodality problem first described for non-autoregressive translation [Gu et al. 2018].

We measured this on 602 pre-registered bimodal pairs (9,632 prompts, eight templates in both orders) that were never used during exploration. At the deployed sampler settings (32 steps, guidance 8, noise 0.1), the 0.5B model produced cross-answer hybrids such as "San York" in 7.1 per cent of prompts, against 0.1 per cent for the same weights decoding autoregressively. The paired difference of 6.97 percentage points [6.09, 7.95] exceeded the pre-registered 5-point margin (Holm-corrected, one-sided p < 5 × 10⁻⁵). With SC switched off, the hybrid rate doubled to 14.2 per cent, and outputs that were neither valid nor hybrid rose from 10.2 to 40.7 per cent. SC moved outputs up a ladder from garbled to hybrid to valid far more often than down it (net upward transitions per prompt 0.387 [0.367, 0.407]).

At 7B the clean hybrid rate with SC fell to 0.2 per cent and no longer exceeded the autoregressive baseline by the margin (S1, not supported). The failure itself did not disappear. Without SC, 32.2 per cent of 7B answers were invalid, compared with 9.2 per cent with SC, so the larger model depends on the same repair; it produces fewer clean hybrids and more diffuse garbles when the repair is removed. The headline phenomenon is therefore not "flow models mix answers" but "parallel positions decoded without coordination produce inconsistent joint outputs", of which hybrids are the most legible form.

The second classic symptom, duplicated tokens, appeared in the same data. Across the 2,000 confirmation documents of Section 5, the rate at which a position repeated its left neighbour's token was 2.6 to 2.9 per cent in decompositions where the position could not read its neighbours' fed-back predictions, and 1.1 to 1.8 per cent where it could (difference −1.4 percentage points [−1.9, −0.9]; exploratory re-analysis).

## 4. Self-conditioning repairs in-block coherence

We next tested whether SC makes the tokens of a block depend on one another as text does. Our pre-registered estimand, dep_j, is the pointwise mutual information between the token at position j and its in-block predecessors, estimated from 16 sampled predecessor sequences under Lafa's own autoregressive head. Averaged over positions 2 and 3, SC raised dep by 0.540 nats [0.477, 0.604] on 7,100 held-out natural-text documents, against a margin of 0.10. Because dep is scored by the model itself, we pre-registered an external co-requirement: Mistral-7B-Instruct, which shares no weights with Lafa, assigned SC outputs 1.34 nats [1.24, 1.43] more log-probability per token at positions 2 and 3.

Two alternative explanations make SC's benefit a by-product of the sampler rather than of the feedback channel. Classifier-free guidance could amplify whatever SC contributes, and the low inference noise could make SC redundant or irrelevant. Neither holds. The SC effect on dep was equivalent across guidance strengths 1 and 8 (interaction −0.002 [−0.164, 0.162], within the pre-registered ±0.25 band; TOST p = 0.0015) and non-inferior across inference noise 0.1 and 4.0 (difference 0.027 [−0.196, 0.250]; p = 0.025). The repair also matters downstream. On reserved slices of GSM8K, IFEval and HumanEval, SC improved accuracy by 5.4 percentage points [1.3, 9.7] on average in the regime we use for mechanism analysis, and its coherence gain there was 1.5 times (own-AR judge) and 1.0 times (Mistral) the gain at deployed settings.

## 5. The repair runs through neighbours, not through each position's own feedback

SC gives every position its own previous prediction as an extra input, embedded as an expected token embedding. A position can benefit from this in three ways: from its own fed-back prediction (O), by reading its neighbours' fed-back predictions through attention (N), or because its neighbours' states evolve better when they themselves receive feedback (F). We separate the three routes with a 2 × 2 × 2 intervention that runs two forward passes per denoising step and routes, for a target position j, which pass supplies its own input, which pass supplies the keys and values it reads from each neighbour, and which pass the neighbours' own outputs come from (Section 2.4). The two corner cells reproduce normal SC decoding and SC-off decoding exactly; every one of the 2,000 confirmation blocks passed both identity checks.

Neighbour reading carries the effect. On 2,000 held-out documents, the pre-registered contrast N − 0.20·T on dep was 0.515 [0.409, 0.624] (Holm-corrected one-sided p < 5 × 10⁻⁵), with T = 0.754 [0.591, 0.917] the total SC effect. The Shapley share of N was 1.14 for dep and 0.99 for coherence; values slightly above one reflect small negative contributions from the other routes rather than an effect larger than the total. The same decomposition under the external judge gave a Möbius N share of 0.80 [0.72, 0.91] and a Shapley share of 1.04 [0.95, 1.14], so the result does not depend on scoring with Lafa's own head. With guidance on, at deployed settings, the N share was 0.73 and the contrast 0.440 [0.302, 0.584] (pre-registered secondary S12).

A position's own feedback contributes nothing and can hurt. Its Shapley share under the external judge was −0.09 [−0.17, −0.03]. Removing only the target's own feedback while keeping its neighbours' improved dep by 0.134 [0.028, 0.240] and coherence by 0.230 [0.118, 0.338] in the mechanism regime, though not at deployed settings (dep −0.04, coherence −0.14), so we treat the harm as regime-dependent.

To test whether this division of labour is a property of SC itself rather than of Lafa, we trained small blockwise flow models from scratch on a task in which each prompt has exactly two valid four-token answers. We routed SC exactly, at training and at inference, so that a position received only its own feedback, only its neighbours', both, or neither. Over three seeds per variant, own-only SC reached 30 per cent valid answers, indistinguishable from no SC (26 per cent). Neighbour-only SC reached 72 per cent (range 71 to 75), above full SC at 53 per cent, and cut hybrids from 72 to 25 per cent. A model given every opportunity to learn to use its own feedback does not, while a model limited to its neighbours' feedback learns to coordinate better than one given both.

## 6. Feedback substitutes for neighbours' states while those states are uninformative

The decomposition says which route carries the benefit, not when or why. We cut neighbour feedback in windows of denoising steps and bands of layers on 300 exploration documents. Under Lafa's default schedule, 74 per cent of the effect [57, 94] sat in the last quarter of the 32 steps and almost none in the first, while across layers it was spread over the middle of the network, with layers 8 to 11 the largest band (19 per cent) and layer 8 alone carrying 20 per cent [9, 32]. The final step alone carried 5 per cent, so the timing is not an artefact of where the output is read.

Three accounts predict this late timing and make different predictions when the schedule changes. If coordination happens where the output is read, it stays at the final steps under any schedule. If the network gates feedback reading on the noise level, it follows the noise level. If feedback stands in for neighbours' states that are still too noisy to read, it follows the point at which those states become informative. Lafa's default schedule keeps noise high until a final jump, which all three accounts accommodate. We therefore reran the step map under two alternative schedules, committing the predictions and decision rule to the public repository before running.

Under a schedule that never leaves the high-noise range, the effect stayed at the end (95 per cent in the last quarter). Under a uniform schedule whose states become clean partway, it moved to steps 8 to 15 (63 per cent) and was exactly zero in the last quarter, where the states are clean. Both the readout and the gating accounts fail one of these predictions.

A control then refined the remaining account. If feedback merely substitutes for states, corrupting the neighbours' states while keeping their feedback should hurt late in the uniform schedule. It did not: once states were clean, either channel alone sufficed, and only removing both hurt. Under the default schedule, removing the feedback, removing the state and removing both hurt by 0.81, 1.31 and 3.02 nats respectively, more than the sum of the single cuts. Because this two-channel account was formulated after the control, we pre-registered three tests of it and ran them on 1,000 confirmation documents never used before. All three were supported (Holm-corrected): under the default schedule the redundancy index was 0.553 [0.315, 0.791]; under the uniform schedule late in decoding, cutting feedback alone changed dep by 0.000 (upper bound 0.004) while removing both channels cost 0.206 [0.141, 0.276]; and the feedback effect was 0.539 [0.414, 0.671] larger at middle than at late uniform steps.

The confirmed account is that a position learns what its neighbours will say through two redundant channels, their states and their fed-back predictions, and that the feedback is necessary exactly when the state channel cannot supply that information. Lafa's default schedule keeps states uninformative until the final jump, which is why coordination there runs through the feedback in the late steps.

## 7. A distributed set of heads whose removal produces the failure

We localised the neighbour reading at the level of attention heads, pre-registering the procedure and its two tests before any of the reserved circuit-validation data were generated. Single-head cuts over the 126 heads of the nine strongest layers identified no dominant head: the largest, L19H3, carried 5 per cent of the full effect, followed by L9H13, L8H8 and L8H7. We therefore cut the top-ranked heads jointly and evaluated on 200 exploration documents not used for ranking. The joint share rose from 26 per cent for the top 10 to 51 per cent for the top 26, and we froze H\*, the smallest set reaching half the effect, at those 26 heads, 7.7 per cent of the model's 336.

Behaviourally, H\* produces the failure the paper began with. On all 1,888 circuit-validation bimodal prompts, decoded at deployed settings, cutting the feedback reads of H\* raised the rate of invalid answers from 17.6 to 48.8 per cent, while 20 random head sets matched to H\* in size and layer distribution raised it only to 20.0 per cent. The difference of 28.9 percentage points [24.6, 33.3] exceeded the pre-registered 3-point margin (L2, supported). Hybrids rose from 6.2 to 11.6 per cent and other invalid outputs from 11.3 to 37.2 per cent, with outputs such as "Franz Kafka" becoming "Fran King" and "Chinua Achebe" becoming "Chin Gaiman".

The mechanistic share test was inconclusive by a narrow margin. H\* removed 43.8 per cent of the neighbour-reading effect against 6.1 per cent for the random sets, a difference of 0.377 [0.298, 0.454] against a pre-registered bar of 0.30. Before generating these data we committed to deciding this test at a one-sided α of 0.025 regardless of the behavioural test; under that rule its lower bound of 0.298 misses the bar. Under the step-down procedure originally pre-registered for the pair, the supported behavioural test would have released the share test to α = 0.05, where its lower bound is 0.311. We report the stricter outcome. The coordination is carried by a distributed but specific set of heads, fewer than one in twelve of the model's heads, whose removal nearly triples cross-answer and garbled outputs.

Finally, editing the messages alone reproduces the failure on demand. On 200 exploration prompts we let a position read its neighbours' fed-back predictions from a different run that had committed to the other answer, while leaving every state of the original run in place. The position switched to the donor answer's token in 39.5 per cent of cases, against 0.5 per cent for a donor that had committed to the same answer (difference 39.0 points [32.0, 45.5]). Transplanting only in the late window was nearly as effective (37.0 per cent); transplanting only in the early window had no effect (0.5 per cent). Of the 79 switches, 77 produced hybrids that kept the original first word and took the donor's second, such as "Key West" becoming "Key Jose" and "San Antonio" becoming "San Lauderdale". These predictions were committed before the run.
