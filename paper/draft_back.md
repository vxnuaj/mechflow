## 9. Related work

**Self-conditioning.** Chen et al. [2022] introduced self-conditioning for diffusion on discrete data, and Dieleman et al. [2022] and Strudel et al. [2022] carried it into continuous language diffusion. Recent work proposes several explanations: a correction of the train/inference mismatch together with improved token identifiability [Davis et al. 2026], fixed-point refinement [Yoo et al. 2026], avoidance of low-density regions in a critical time interval [Shabalin et al. 2026], and redundancy with the information a solver has already folded into the latent [Li & Xu 2026]. Each is a per-position or temporal account. Pynadath et al. [2025] remark that previous predictions may act as anchors for denoising other positions, the closest prior statement of our finding, which we turn into a causal and pre-registered measurement. Cardei et al. [2026] report that SC's benefit grows with backbone capability across backbones; our scale comparison is controlled within one model family. Zhang et al. [2026] intervene causally on a self-conditioning direction to reduce repetition; we decompose SC's coherence benefit by route.

**Coordination in parallel decoding.** The multimodality problem of non-autoregressive translation [Gu et al. 2018] and its iterative-refinement remedies [Ghazvininejad et al. 2019] frame our failure modes. Recent work on parallel decoding in diffusion language models studies factorisation error and commitment [Ji et al. 2026; Gushchin et al. 2026; Kulkarni et al. 2026], and He et al. [2026] show causally that masked diffusion models reason jointly about undecoded positions within a forward pass. We isolate a different channel: communication across denoising steps through fed-back predictions.

**Interpretability of diffusion language models.** Engels et al. [2026] characterise token smearing and commitment in DiffusionGemma, a masked diffusion model whose self-conditioning matrix has the same softmax-times-embedding form as Lafa's, and pose activation patching across denoising steps and circuit analysis of non-autoregressive behaviour as open problems. They also find that most information in the intermediate vectors can be ablated with little loss, a contrast we discuss in Section 10. Catruna & Radoi [2026] localise previous-token, next-token and bidirectional induction heads in small masked models. Our methods extend activation and path patching [Wang et al. 2023; Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2023] to a recurrent edge that runs through the sampler.

## 10. Discussion

Self-conditioning in Lafa behaves as a message channel between positions. Each position broadcasts its current prediction; its neighbours read that prediction through a distributed set of heads in layers 3 to 19; and they rely on it exactly when the neighbours' states are too noisy to read directly. This account explains the four observations that a per-position account does not: the null contribution of a position's own feedback, the late timing under the default schedule and its movement under other schedules, the super-additive loss when both the state and the feedback channel are removed, and the hybrids produced by transplanting only the messages. It also connects SC to the oldest failure of non-autoregressive decoding. Independent per-position choices produce mixtures and duplicates [Gu et al. 2018], and SC supplies the conditional information that independent decoding lacks without giving up parallel updates.

The account suggests design choices that we have not yet tested at scale. If own feedback is redundant with a position's state, as both the decomposition and the trained toy models indicate, a variant that feeds back only neighbours' predictions removes a route that can hurt coherence; in the toy model it raised valid answers from 53 to 72 per cent, and in Lafa removing own feedback improved coherence in the mechanism regime but not at deployed settings. Schedules that keep states uninformative until late make decoding depend on the feedback channel for longer, which may explain why SC matters more for some samplers than others.

The contrast with DiffusionGemma, whose intermediate vectors can be heavily compressed with little loss [Engels et al. 2026], is not a contradiction. Their ablation reduces the information each fed-back vector carries; ours removes a position's access to its neighbours' vectors altogether. A low-dimensional message can suffice for coordination, which is what the two-channel account predicts when the main content to transmit is which of a few answers a neighbour is committing to.

Several limits bound these conclusions. The mechanism is confirmed in one model family at 0.5B and, pending the pre-registered test, explored at 7B, where a position's own feedback begins to contribute a quarter of the effect; the second-architecture test is pending. Most mechanism analyses run without guidance; the neighbour share remains the majority with guidance on (0.73), but it is smaller. The head-share test missed its pre-registered bar by 0.002 under the stricter rule we committed to, so we claim the behavioural role of the 26 heads, which passed decisively, and report their share descriptively. Our dependence measure is noisy at 7B, which is why the 7B test uses coherence under two judges as its primary outcome.
