# Neighbours, Not Selves: How Self-Conditioning Coordinates Parallel Decoding in Flow Language Models

## Abstract

Continuous flow and diffusion language models decode several tokens in parallel, and almost all of them rely on self-conditioning, which feeds each position's previous prediction back as an input at the next denoising step. Self-conditioning is known to improve sample quality, but why it does so remains unexplained. We study Lafa, a blockwise flow language model, with causal interventions that route the fed-back predictions through attention. Without self-conditioning, parallel positions decoded independently produce inconsistent joint outputs, including cross-answer hybrids such as "San York" and duplicated tokens. A pre-registered decomposition on held-out data shows that the repair runs almost entirely through each position reading its neighbours' fed-back predictions rather than its own: the neighbour route carries 0.99 to 1.14 of the effect at 0.5B under two independent judges, and a position's own feedback contributes nothing or slightly harms coherence. Changing the sampler's noise schedule moves this effect in time, and a pre-registered test on 1,000 further documents confirms that positions learn their neighbours' content through two redundant channels, the neighbours' states and their fed-back predictions, with the feedback necessary exactly while the states are uninformative. Cutting the feedback reads of 26 attention heads, fewer than one in twelve, nearly triples cross-answer and garbled outputs at deployed settings, while matched random heads do not, and transplanting neighbours' fed-back predictions from another run creates hybrids on demand. A model trained from scratch with neighbour-only self-conditioning matches or exceeds full self-conditioning, while own-only self-conditioning is no better than none. Self-conditioning in parallel decoders is therefore best understood as a learned message channel between positions.

## 1. Introduction

Parallel decoding is the main promise of non-autoregressive language models: a block of tokens is denoised jointly over a fixed number of steps rather than generated one token at a time [Gu et al. 2018; Ghazvininejad et al. 2019]. Its main hazard is also old. When positions predict their tokens without seeing what their neighbours will commit to, the product of per-position marginals can assign high probability to combinations no single answer contains, the multimodality problem first described for non-autoregressive translation [Gu et al. 2018]. Recent continuous flow and diffusion language models reach competitive quality at scale [Chavan et al. 2026; Yang et al. 2026], and nearly all of them use self-conditioning (SC), in which each position receives its own previous prediction as an extra input [Chen et al. 2022; Dieleman et al. 2022; Strudel et al. 2022].

Why SC helps is an open question. Existing accounts treat it as a per-position or temporal mechanism: it corrects a train/inference mismatch and improves token identifiability [Davis et al. 2026], implements fixed-point refinement [Yoo et al. 2026], keeps trajectories out of low-density regions [Shabalin et al. 2026], or repeats information the solver has already folded into the latent [Li & Xu 2026]. Pynadath et al. [2025] suggest, without testing, that previous predictions may serve as anchors for other positions. These accounts make different predictions about where SC's benefit comes from, but none separates the routes by intervention.

We answer the question causally in Lafa [Chavan et al. 2026], a blockwise flow language model that denoises four tokens at a time. We first confirm on held-out data that, without SC, parallel decoding fails in both classic ways, and that SC repairs in-block coherence under the model's own judge and an external one. We then decompose SC's benefit into three routes with a 2 × 2 × 2 intervention that controls, for a target position, whether it receives its own fed-back prediction, whether it reads its neighbours' fed-back predictions through attention, and whether the neighbours' own trajectories receive feedback. Every confirmatory claim was pre-registered, with its hash anchored in a public repository before the data that test it existed, and every deviation was logged before the affected data; exploratory results are labelled as such.

Our contributions are as follows.

- **Neighbours, not selves.** On 2,000 held-out documents, the neighbour-reading route carries 0.99 to 1.14 of SC's coherence and dependence benefit at 0.5B (Shapley share; external judge 1.04), while a position's own feedback contributes nothing or slightly harms (Section 5). The same division appears in a model trained from scratch, where neighbour-only SC matches or exceeds full SC and own-only SC is no better than none.
- **When and why.** The effect sits in the late denoising steps under Lafa's default schedule because that schedule keeps states uninformative until a final jump. Changing the schedule moves the effect, and a pre-registered test on 1,000 further documents confirms a two-channel account in which states and fed-back predictions carry the same neighbour information redundantly (Section 6).
- **Where.** The reading is distributed across layers 3 to 19. Cutting the neighbour-feedback reads of 26 heads nearly triples cross-answer and garbled outputs at deployed settings, against no effect for matched random heads; these heads account for 44 per cent of the neighbour effect against 6 per cent for random sets, short of our pre-registered margin (Section 7).
- **Steering by message transplant.** Replacing only the neighbours' fed-back predictions with those of a run that committed to the other answer produces hybrids on demand, and only when done in the late window (Section 7).
- **Methods.** A causal decomposition of a recurrent feedback channel, per-query key/value routing for patching through the denoising-time feedback edge, and schedule manipulation as a falsification tool for mechanistic accounts (Section 2).

Section 2 describes Lafa, self-conditioning, our measures and the interventions. Section 3 establishes the failure that SC repairs and Section 4 the repair. Sections 5 to 7 give the mechanism, its timing and its localisation. Section 8 tests generality at 7B and in a second architecture, Section 9 places the results in the literature and Section 10 discusses implications and limits.

## 2. Setting, measures and interventions

**Model and decoding.** Lafa decodes a response in blocks of four positions. Within a block every position starts from Gaussian noise in token-embedding space and is denoised over K = 32 Euler steps with bidirectional attention inside the block and causal attention to the prefix. Self-conditioning modifies the input of position i at step k to x̃_i = x_i + W₁x_i + W₂(q_i E), where x_i is the noisy state, q_i is the position's predicted distribution from step k − 1 and E is the embedding matrix. Lafa trains with SC dropped for the whole batch with probability 0.5, so SC-off decoding (q E replaced by zero) is in-distribution. The model's output is the final step's argmax prediction. We use the 0.5B checkpoint for all confirmatory tests and the 7B checkpoint for scale.

**Regimes.** Lafa is deployed with classifier-free prefix guidance g = 8 and inference noise 0.1, the paper regime (PR). We run mechanism analyses in a regime without guidance and with the training noise scale 4.0, the mechanism regime (MR), because guidance introduces a second feedback state per branch. We test each mechanistic claim in PR as well. The SC effect is equivalent across the two guidance settings and across the two noise settings (Section 4), and SC's end-task benefit and coherence gain are present in MR.

**Measures.** For position j of a generated block we define coherence, coh_j = log p(y_j | c, y_<j), under Lafa's own autoregressive head, and in-block dependence, dep_j = coh_j − log of the mean probability of y_j under 16 predecessor sequences sampled from the same head. dep_j measures how much the token depends on the specific predecessors the block produced. Because both measures use Lafa's own head, we also score every output with Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, which shares no weights with Lafa. For bimodal prompts (choose one of two named entities) we classify each answer as one of the two valid answers, a hybrid formed from a prefix of one and a suffix of the other at token boundaries, or other; the classifier agreed with a 150-label audit on 98.7 per cent of items and on all hybrids.

**The 2 × 2 × 2 decomposition.** At every denoising step we run two forward passes on the same states: pass A with SC at a chosen set of positions and pass B with SC at another. For target j, three binary factors select (O) whether j's own input carries its feedback, (N) whether j's attention reads its neighbours' keys and values from the pass in which they carry feedback, and (F) whether the neighbours' own outputs, and hence their next states and feedback, come from that pass. Per-query key/value routing is implemented by appending both passes' keys and values to the cache and masking each query's reads. The (1,1,1) and (0,0,0) cells reproduce normal SC-on and SC-off decoding token for token, and we check both identities on every block. We report Möbius main effects, Shapley shares and the pre-registered linear contrast N − 0.20·T, where T is the total SC effect.

**Localisation and steering.** Head-level cuts replace a target's reads of its neighbours' keys and values, at chosen heads, layers and steps, with those from a pass in which the neighbours receive no feedback; all compared conditions run through the same code path. For steering we record a donor run's fed-back predictions at every step and let the target read its neighbours through (recipient states, donor feedback).

**Pre-registration and analysis.** The primary family (hybrid excess, SC repair, neighbour share) was tested with Holm correction over three tests and cluster bootstrap intervals with 20,000 resamples; secondary tests used Benjamini–Hochberg control at q = 0.10. Later families (the two-channel account, the 7B mechanism, the second architecture and the head tests) were each pre-registered with their own decision rules before their data existed. Exploratory analyses are labelled as such throughout.

## 3. Parallel decoding fails in two ways without self-conditioning

We first establish the phenomenon that self-conditioning (SC) has to solve. Lafa generates each four-token block in parallel, so every position predicts its token without seeing the tokens its neighbours will finally commit to. When a prompt admits two valid answers, independent per-position choices can combine into an answer that belongs to neither, the multimodality problem first described for non-autoregressive translation [Gu et al. 2018].

We measured this on 602 pre-registered bimodal pairs (9,632 prompts, eight templates in both orders) that were never used during exploration. At the deployed sampler settings (32 steps, guidance 8, noise 0.1), the 0.5B model produced cross-answer hybrids such as "San York" in 7.1 per cent of prompts, against 0.1 per cent for the same weights decoding autoregressively. The paired difference of 6.97 percentage points [6.09, 7.95] exceeded the pre-registered 5-point margin (Holm-corrected, one-sided p < 5 × 10⁻⁵). With SC switched off, the hybrid rate doubled to 14.2 per cent, and outputs that were neither valid nor hybrid rose from 10.2 to 40.7 per cent. SC moved outputs up a ladder from garbled to hybrid to valid far more often than down it (net upward transitions per prompt 0.387 [0.367, 0.407]).

At 7B the clean hybrid rate with SC fell to 0.2 per cent and no longer exceeded the autoregressive baseline by the margin (S1, not supported). The failure itself did not disappear. Without SC, 32.2 per cent of 7B answers were invalid, compared with 9.2 per cent with SC, so the larger model depends on the same repair; it produces fewer clean hybrids and more diffuse garbles when the repair is removed. The headline phenomenon is therefore not "flow models mix answers" but "parallel positions decoded without coordination produce inconsistent joint outputs", of which hybrids are the most legible form.

The second classic symptom, duplicated tokens, appeared in the same data. Across the 2,000 confirmation documents of Section 5, the rate at which a position repeated its left neighbour's token was 2.6 to 2.9 per cent in decompositions where the position could not read its neighbours' fed-back predictions, and 1.1 to 1.8 per cent where it could (difference −1.4 percentage points [−1.9, −0.9]; exploratory re-analysis).

## 4. Self-conditioning repairs in-block coherence

We next tested whether SC makes the tokens of a block depend on one another as text does. Our pre-registered estimand, dep_j, is the pointwise mutual information between the token at position j and its in-block predecessors, estimated from 16 sampled predecessor sequences under Lafa's own autoregressive head. Averaged over positions 2 and 3, SC raised dep by 0.540 nats [0.477, 0.604] on 7,100 held-out natural-text documents, against a margin of 0.10. Because dep is scored by the model itself, we pre-registered an external co-requirement: Mistral-7B-Instruct, which shares no weights with Lafa, assigned SC outputs 1.34 nats [1.24, 1.43] more log-probability per token at positions 2 and 3.

Two alternative explanations make SC's benefit a by-product of the sampler rather than of the feedback channel. Classifier-free guidance could amplify whatever SC contributes, and the low inference noise could make SC redundant or irrelevant. Neither holds. The SC effect on dep was equivalent across guidance strengths 1 and 8 (interaction −0.002 [−0.164, 0.162], within the pre-registered ±0.25 band; TOST p = 0.0015) and non-inferior across inference noise 0.1 and 4.0 (difference 0.027 [−0.196, 0.250]; p = 0.025). The repair also matters downstream. On reserved slices of GSM8K, IFEval and HumanEval, SC improved accuracy by 5.4 percentage points [1.3, 9.7] on average in the regime we use for mechanism analysis, and its coherence gain there was 1.5 times (own-AR judge) and 1.0 times (Mistral) the gain at deployed settings.

## 5. The repair runs through neighbours, not through each position's own feedback

SC gives every position its own previous prediction as an extra input, embedded as an expected token embedding. A position can benefit from this in three ways: from its own fed-back prediction (O), by reading its neighbours' fed-back predictions through attention (N), or because its neighbours' states evolve better when they themselves receive feedback (F). We separate the three routes with a 2 × 2 × 2 intervention that runs two forward passes per denoising step and routes, for a target position j, which pass supplies its own input, which pass supplies the keys and values it reads from each neighbour, and which pass the neighbours' own outputs come from (Section 2.4). The two corner cells reproduce normal SC decoding and SC-off decoding exactly; every one of the 2,000 confirmation blocks passed both identity checks.

Neighbour reading carries the effect. On 2,000 held-out documents, the pre-registered contrast N − 0.20·T on dep was 0.515 [0.409, 0.624] (Holm-corrected one-sided p < 5 × 10⁻⁵), with T = 0.754 [0.591, 0.917] the total SC effect. The Shapley share of N was 1.14 for dep and 0.99 for coherence; values slightly above one reflect small negative contributions from the other routes rather than an effect larger than the total. The same decomposition under the external judge gave a Möbius N share of 0.80 [0.72, 0.91] and a Shapley share of 1.04 [0.95, 1.14], so the result does not depend on scoring with Lafa's own head. With guidance on, at deployed settings, the N share was 0.73 and the contrast 0.440 [0.302, 0.584] (pre-registered secondary S12).

A position's own feedback contributes nothing and can hurt. Its Shapley share under the external judge was −0.09 [−0.17, −0.03]. Removing only the target's own feedback while keeping its neighbours' improved dep by 0.134 [0.028, 0.240] and coherence by 0.230 [0.118, 0.338] in the mechanism regime, though not at deployed settings (dep −0.04, coherence −0.14), so we treat the harm as regime-dependent.

To test whether this division of labour is a property of SC itself rather than of Lafa, we trained small blockwise flow models from scratch on a task in which each prompt has exactly two valid four-token answers. We routed SC exactly, at training and at inference, so that a position received only its own feedback, only its neighbours', both, or neither. Over three seeds per variant, own-only SC reached 30 per cent valid answers, indistinguishable from no SC (26 per cent). Neighbour-only SC reached 72 per cent (range 71 to 75), above full SC at 53 per cent, and cut hybrids from 72 to 25 per cent. A model given every opportunity to learn to use its own feedback does not, while a model limited to its neighbours' feedback learns to coordinate better than one given both.

## 6. Feedback substitutes for neighbours' states while those states are uninformative

The decomposition says which route carries the benefit, not when or why. We cut neighbour feedback in windows of denoising steps and bands of layers on 300 exploration documents. Under Lafa's default schedule, 74 per cent of the effect [57, 94] sat in the last quarter of the 32 steps and almost none in the first, while across layers it was spread over the middle of the network, with layers 8 to 11 the largest band (19 per cent) and layer 8 alone carrying 20 per cent [9, 32]. The final step alone carried 5 per cent, so the timing is not an artefact of where the output is read.

Three accounts predict this late timing and make different predictions when the schedule changes. If coordination happens where the output is read, it stays at the final steps under any schedule. If the network gates feedback reading on the noise level, it follows the noise level. If feedback stands in for neighbours' states that are still too noisy to read, it follows the point at which those states become informative. Lafa's default schedule keeps noise high until a final jump, which all three accounts accommodate. We therefore reran the step map under two alternative schedules, committing the predictions and decision rule to the public repository before running.

Under a schedule that never leaves the high-noise range, the effect stayed at the end (95 per cent in the last quarter). Under a uniform schedule whose states become clean partway, it moved to steps 8 to 15 (63 per cent) and was exactly zero in the last quarter, where the states are clean. Both the readout and the gating accounts fail one of these predictions.

A control then refined the remaining account. If feedback merely substitutes for states, corrupting the neighbours' states while keeping their feedback should hurt late in the uniform schedule. It did not: once states were clean, either channel alone sufficed, and only removing both hurt. Under the default schedule, removing the feedback, removing the state and removing both hurt by 0.81, 1.31 and 3.02 nats respectively, more than the sum of the single cuts. Because this two-channel account was formulated after the control, we pre-registered three tests of it and ran them on 1,000 confirmation documents never used before. All three were supported (Holm-corrected): under the default schedule the redundancy index was 0.553 [0.315, 0.791]; under the uniform schedule late in decoding, cutting feedback alone changed dep by 0.000 (upper bound 0.004) while removing both channels cost 0.206 [0.141, 0.276]; and the feedback effect was 0.539 [0.414, 0.671] larger at middle than at late uniform steps.

The confirmed account is that a position learns what its neighbours will say through two redundant channels, their states and their fed-back predictions, and that the feedback is necessary exactly when the state channel cannot supply that information. Lafa's default schedule keeps states uninformative until the final jump, which is why coordination there runs through the feedback in the late steps.

## 7. A distributed set of heads whose removal produces the failure

We localised the neighbour reading at the level of attention heads, pre-registering the procedure and its two tests before any of the reserved circuit-validation data were generated. Single-head cuts over the 126 heads of the nine strongest layers identified no dominant head: the largest, L19H3, carried 5 per cent of the full effect, followed by L9H13, L8H8 and L8H7. We therefore cut the top-ranked heads jointly and evaluated on 200 exploration documents not used for ranking. The joint share rose from 26 per cent for the top 10 to 51 per cent for the top 26, and we froze H\*, the smallest set reaching half the effect, at those 26 heads, 7.7 per cent of the model's 336.

Behaviourally, H\* produces the failure the paper began with. On all 1,888 circuit-validation bimodal prompts, decoded at deployed settings, cutting the feedback reads of H\* raised the rate of invalid answers from 17.6 to 48.8 per cent, while 20 random head sets matched to H\* in size and layer distribution raised it only to 20.0 per cent. The difference of 28.9 percentage points [24.6, 33.3] exceeded the pre-registered 3-point margin (L2, supported). Hybrids rose from 6.2 to 11.6 per cent and other invalid outputs from 11.3 to 37.2 per cent, with outputs such as "Franz Kafka" becoming "Fran King" and "Chinua Achebe" becoming "Chin Gaiman".

The mechanistic share test was inconclusive by a narrow margin. H\* removed 43.8 per cent of the neighbour-reading effect against 6.1 per cent for the random sets, a difference of 0.377 [0.298, 0.454] against a pre-registered bar of 0.30. Before generating these data we committed to deciding this test at a one-sided α of 0.025 regardless of the behavioural test; under that rule its lower bound of 0.298 misses the bar. Under the step-down procedure originally pre-registered for the pair, the supported behavioural test would have released the share test to α = 0.05, where its lower bound is 0.311. We report the stricter outcome. The coordination is carried by a distributed but specific set of heads, fewer than one in twelve of the model's heads, whose removal nearly triples cross-answer and garbled outputs.

Finally, editing the messages alone reproduces the failure on demand. On 200 exploration prompts we let a position read its neighbours' fed-back predictions from a different run that had committed to the other answer, while leaving every state of the original run in place. The position switched to the donor answer's token in 39.5 per cent of cases, against 0.5 per cent for a donor that had committed to the same answer (difference 39.0 points [32.0, 45.5]). Transplanting only in the late window was nearly as effective (37.0 per cent); transplanting only in the early window had no effect (0.5 per cent). Of the 79 switches, 77 produced hybrids that kept the original first word and took the donor's second, such as "Key West" becoming "Key Jose" and "San Antonio" becoming "San Lauderdale". These predictions were committed before the run.

## 9. Related work

**Self-conditioning.** Chen et al. [2022] introduced self-conditioning for diffusion on discrete data, and Dieleman et al. [2022] and Strudel et al. [2022] carried it into continuous language diffusion. Recent work proposes several explanations: a correction of the train/inference mismatch together with improved token identifiability [Davis et al. 2026], fixed-point refinement [Yoo et al. 2026], avoidance of low-density regions in a critical time interval [Shabalin et al. 2026], and redundancy with the information a solver has already folded into the latent [Li & Xu 2026]. Each is a per-position or temporal account. Pynadath et al. [2025] remark that previous predictions may act as anchors for denoising other positions, the closest prior statement of our finding, which we turn into a causal and pre-registered measurement. Cardei et al. [2026] report that SC's benefit grows with backbone capability across backbones; our scale comparison is controlled within one model family. Zhang et al. [2026] intervene causally on a self-conditioning direction to reduce repetition; we decompose SC's coherence benefit by route.

**Coordination in parallel decoding.** The multimodality problem of non-autoregressive translation [Gu et al. 2018] and its iterative-refinement remedies [Ghazvininejad et al. 2019] frame our failure modes. Recent work on parallel decoding in diffusion language models studies factorisation error and commitment [Ji et al. 2026; Gushchin et al. 2026; Kulkarni et al. 2026], and He et al. [2026] show causally that masked diffusion models reason jointly about undecoded positions within a forward pass. We isolate a different channel: communication across denoising steps through fed-back predictions.

**Interpretability of diffusion language models.** Engels et al. [2026] characterise token smearing and commitment in DiffusionGemma, a masked diffusion model whose self-conditioning matrix has the same softmax-times-embedding form as Lafa's, and pose activation patching across denoising steps and circuit analysis of non-autoregressive behaviour as open problems. They also find that most information in the intermediate vectors can be ablated with little loss, a contrast we discuss in Section 10. Catruna & Radoi [2026] localise previous-token, next-token and bidirectional induction heads in small masked models. Our methods extend activation and path patching [Wang et al. 2023; Goldowsky-Dill et al. 2023] to a recurrent edge that runs through the sampler.

## 10. Discussion

Self-conditioning in Lafa behaves as a message channel between positions. Each position broadcasts its current prediction; its neighbours read that prediction through a distributed set of heads in layers 3 to 19; and they rely on it exactly when the neighbours' states are too noisy to read directly. This account explains the four observations that a per-position account does not: the null contribution of a position's own feedback, the late timing under the default schedule and its movement under other schedules, the super-additive loss when both the state and the feedback channel are removed, and the hybrids produced by transplanting only the messages. It also connects SC to the oldest failure of non-autoregressive decoding. Independent per-position choices produce mixtures and duplicates [Gu et al. 2018], and SC supplies the conditional information that independent decoding lacks without giving up parallel updates.

The account suggests design choices that we have not yet tested at scale. If own feedback is redundant with a position's state, as both the decomposition and the trained toy models indicate, a variant that feeds back only neighbours' predictions removes a route that can hurt coherence; in the toy model it raised valid answers from 53 to 72 per cent, and in Lafa removing own feedback improved coherence in the mechanism regime but not at deployed settings. Schedules that keep states uninformative until late make decoding depend on the feedback channel for longer, which may explain why SC matters more for some samplers than others.

The contrast with DiffusionGemma, whose intermediate vectors can be heavily compressed with little loss [Engels et al. 2026], is not a contradiction. Their ablation reduces the information each fed-back vector carries; ours removes a position's access to its neighbours' vectors altogether. A low-dimensional message can suffice for coordination, which is what the two-channel account predicts when the main content to transmit is which of a few answers a neighbour is committing to.

Several limits bound these conclusions. The mechanism is confirmed in one model family at 0.5B and, pending the pre-registered test, explored at 7B, where a position's own feedback begins to contribute a quarter of the effect; the second-architecture test is pending. Most mechanism analyses run without guidance; the neighbour share remains the majority with guidance on (0.73), but it is smaller. The head-share test missed its pre-registered bar by 0.002 under the stricter rule we committed to, so we claim the behavioural role of the 26 heads, which passed decisively, and report their share descriptively. Our dependence measure is noisy at 7B, which is why the 7B test uses coherence under two judges as its primary outcome.
