# How Do Parallel Positions Agree? Self-Conditioning and Mode Mixing in Continuous-Flow Language Models

**Status:** pre-registered (`prereg.md` v1.3, frozen; external anchor pending). Next: build and validate the instruments (Phase 6). Last updated 2026-11-13.
**Target venue:** ICML 2027 (deadline approx. late January 2027).
**Model studied:** Lafa (Large Flow Language Models; Chavan et al., 2026, arXiv:2610.28559).

---

## 1. Summary

Continuous-flow language models generate several tokens in parallel by integrating a flow from noise toward token embeddings. Lafa, a recent flow LM adapted from Qwen2.5, generates text in blocks of four tokens and uses **self-conditioning (SC)**: at every denoising step, the model's previous prediction is fed back as an extra input. Turning SC off at inference lowers Lafa-0.5B's average accuracy from 38.1% to 32.3%. Why SC helps is not understood. Recent work calls it "poorly understood" and "still unclear".

The project asks two questions:
1. **What failure does SC fix in a parallel flow LM?**
2. **Through which internal channel does the fix act?** Do positions in a block read each other's fed-back predictions to agree on one continuation, or does each position simply stabilize its own prediction?

Exploratory experiments so far support three findings:

- **Mode mixing persists in a real flow LM.** On prompts with two valid multi-token answers ("Choose either New York or San Francisco"), Lafa's flow decoding at its published settings produces cross-answer hybrids in about 30% of cases ("San York", "Las Orleans", "Albert Newton"). The **same checkpoint**, decoding with its own autoregressive (AR) head, produces none. To our knowledge this is the first measurement of this failure in a real continuous flow LM. Prior work shows it only in toy examples or for discrete samplers.
- **SC's benefit is within-block coherence, not per-token accuracy.** SC barely changes token-level agreement with reference solutions. It does make generated blocks substantially more coherent: +3.6 nats under the model's own AR head, in blocks where SC changes the output. Almost all of the gain sits at the later block positions, the tokens that must fit what came earlier in the same block.
- **At deployed settings, the state quickly absorbs the feedback.** At Lafa's inference noise level, the evolving latent state carries the same "which answer" information as the fed-back prediction within about two denoising steps. At the training noise level, the fed-back prediction leads the state by about 15–20 steps. This determines where the mechanism can be tested cleanly.

The remaining work decomposes SC's coherence benefit causally into competing channels, using pre-registered interventions on the model's internals.

## 2. Background

### 2.1 Lafa
- Lafa splits text into blocks of B = 4 tokens.
- Each block is generated by integrating a flow from Gaussian noise toward the block's token embeddings, conditioned on the already-generated prefix.
- Attention is bidirectional inside the block and causal over the prefix.
- Each position follows its own learned time schedule, and earlier positions in a block are denoised faster than later ones.
- Inference uses 32 Euler steps, a small initial noise scale (0.1, compared with 4.0 during training), and "prefix guidance" (classifier-free guidance against a prefix-free pass) with scale 8.
- The same checkpoint can also decode autoregressively through a retained AR head.
- Checkpoints: 0.5B, 7B and 8B (Apache-2.0).

### 2.2 Self-conditioning
At each step, the denoiser's input at every position is the current latent state plus a learned projection of the previous step's predicted token distribution (its expected embedding). During training, this feedback is computed from an extra no-gradient pass and used in half of the training steps. In the other half the plain state is used. Switching SC off at inference therefore returns the model to a condition it was trained on.

### 2.3 The puzzle
The feedback enters each position separately, but attention lets every position read every other. SC could therefore help in two ways:
- by improving each position's own prediction (a per-position effect);
- by letting positions read each other's current predictions and agree on one joint continuation (a cross-position, coordination effect).

The second is specific to parallel generation and would explain how flow LMs avoid incoherent mixtures of valid answers. No published work separates these.

## 3. Research Questions and Hypotheses

### 3.1 Phenomenon claims (descriptive)
- **P1.** At Lafa's published decoding settings, flow decoding produces cross-mode hybrids on two-answer prompts at a substantial rate, while the same checkpoint's AR decoding does not.
- **P2.** SC improves within-block coherence, concentrated at later block positions. This must hold under the model's own AR head, under an external judge model, and under an in-block dependence measure that does not reward mere typicality.
- **P3.** The SC effect is not substantially moderated by guidance strength, and it is not larger at low noise.

### 3.2 Competing mechanisms (causal)
- **M2: feedback-channel coordination (headline candidate).** Within a step, a position uses its neighbours' fed-back predictions, beyond what their latent states already carry, and these predictions carry which answer the neighbour is committing to.
- **R0: state-channel coordination.** Positions coordinate by reading each other's latent states. SC helps only because better predictions produce better states.
- **M1: own-position stabilization.** Each position's own feedback stabilizes its own prediction within a step.
- **M5: own-trajectory closeness** (after Shabalin et al., 2026). Own feedback helps by keeping successive predictions close, which keeps the trajectory out of low-density regions.

These channels are not mutually exclusive. The study estimates the share of SC's benefit carried by each and tests each share against a pre-registered minimum.

### 3.3 Mechanisms considered and set aside (reported as "not supported, underpowered")
- **M3: guidance anchoring.** SC helps because the unguided feedback stabilizes the strongly guided trajectory. Not supported: the SC effect was essentially the same with guidance off.
- **M4: tie-breaking at low noise.** SC helps break ties between answers when the initial noise is small. Not supported: the SC benefit was larger at high noise.

## 4. Results So Far (exploratory; Lafa-0.5B on a laptop GPU)

All results below are from a small exploratory study. The data used are excluded from all later confirmatory tests.

### 4.1 Block-level test (400 teacher-forced blocks from GSM8K solutions)
- Turning SC on barely changes token agreement with the reference solution (+0.5 points; not significant).
- It raises agreement with the model's own AR continuation by +4.5 points per token and +8.3 points per block (95% CI +4.5 to +12.0).
- SC changes the output in 29% of blocks.
- Without SC, outputs typically match the AR continuation for two or three tokens and then drift at the later positions.

### 4.2 Hybrid test (53 prompts asking for two-word entities)

| Condition | Valid answer | Word-level hybrid |
|---|---|---|
| AR head | 89% | 0% |
| Flow with SC (paper settings) | 53% | 28% |
| Flow without SC | 38% | 23% |

- On the "pick A or B" prompts alone, the AR head was 100% valid, while flow with SC produced 30% hybrids.
- Without SC, many outputs mixed answers below the word level ("Albertaac" = Albert + Isaac; "Marileo" = Marie + Galileo).
- SC moved outputs up a ladder, from garbled to word-level hybrid to valid, and never made an output worse. It did not eliminate hybrids.

### 4.3 Coherence (same 400 blocks)
- In blocks where SC changes the output, the SC version scores +3.6 nats higher under the model's own AR head (95% CI +2.4 to +4.9) and is more coherent 73% of the time.
- Per block position, the gain is +0.2, +0.1, +1.5 and +1.8 nats for positions 0 to 3.
- The benefit appears with guidance on and off, and it is larger at the training noise level (+6.4 nats).

### 4.4 Information carried by the feedback vs. the state
For each denoising step, we measured how well the fed-back prediction and the latent state each predict the answer a position will finally output.
- **At inference noise (0.1):** the state matches the feedback's predictiveness within about two steps.
- **At training noise (4.0), with guidance off:** the feedback predicts the final answer with 86–95% accuracy from step 2 onward, while the state stays at 57–75% until about step 20.

The coordination question (M2 vs. R0) is therefore cleanly testable at the training noise level. At inference noise, the feedback can only act on its own during the first few steps.

### 4.5 End-to-end sanity check (50 GSM8K problems)
SC on: 38% accuracy. SC off: 34%. The direction matches the paper, but the sample is too small to be conclusive.

## 5. Novelty and Related Work

- **The hybrid measurement is new.** Hybrid rates have previously been shown only in a toy example (Flow Map Language Models, Lee et al., 2026, which argues continuous flows avoid such errors), in hypothetical examples and discrete-model experiments (CoCommit, 2026; ParallelBench, 2025), and in synthetic settings. No paper measures hybrids in a real continuous flow LM, or compares a flow model's coherence with its own AR head.
- **The mechanism question is open.** Prior accounts of self-conditioning are per-position or across steps:
  - fixed-point refinement (Yoo et al., 2026);
  - keeping trajectories out of low-density regions (Shabalin et al., 2026);
  - correcting a train/inference mismatch (Davis et al., 2026; Li & Xu, 2026);
  - preventing drift toward frequent tokens (LangFlow, 2026).
  
  None tests a cross-position channel or decomposes the self-conditioning gain.
- **Prior causal work on the feedback channel** studied repetition in unconditional generation (Zhang et al., 2026), not coordination.
- **Competition risk:** the group behind Lafa and related flow-map models is active in this exact area. The project monitors new arXiv postings weekly.

## 6. Method (pre-registered design)

### 6.1 Two decoding regimes
- **Paper regime** (noise 0.1, guidance 8, 32 steps): used for the phenomenon claims and end-task numbers. Every intervention is applied to both guidance branches.
- **Mechanism regime** (noise 4.0, guidance 1, 32 steps): the feedback carries information the state lacks for many steps, so the coordination question is testable here. It is also half the compute.
- **Relevance gate:** a mechanism result is headlined only if SC also improves coherence and end-task accuracy in the mechanism regime. Separately, the paper regime is tested within its early-step window.

### 6.2 Outcome measures
- **Own-AR coherence:** the log-probability of each generated token given the prefix and its in-block predecessors, under the model's own AR head.
- **External coherence:** the same quantity under an independent model (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3). Coherence claims require agreement in sign between the two judges.
- **In-block dependence:** how much better a token fits its own predecessors than predecessors sampled from the model. This separates consistency from typicality.
- **Hybrid classification:** each answer span is aligned to the two valid answers and labelled mode, hybrid, other-valid, garble or invalid. The classifier is validated against a manual audit before use.
- **Commitment time and joint flipping:** measured by perturbing intermediate states and checking whether positions change their final tokens independently or together.

### 6.3 Experiments
- **Experiment F (phenomenon at scale):**
  - about 2,600 programmatically generated two-answer prompts and several thousand natural-text blocks;
  - AR vs. flow, SC on and off, varied guidance, noise and step counts;
  - at 0.5B and 7B.
- **Experiment A (direct effect beyond the state):**
  - remove a neighbour's feedback at a single step and measure the effect on a target position;
  - in a variant, first erase the answer information from the neighbour's latent state (linear concept erasure);
  - M2 predicts the feedback still matters after erasure, and R0 predicts it does not.
- **Experiment B (share decomposition):** a 2×2×2 factorial over the whole trajectory. It switches on and off:
  - the target's own feedback;
  - the target's reading of its neighbours' feedback;
  - the neighbours' use of their own feedback.
  
  The resulting interaction terms add up exactly to SC's total effect. They give the share carried by each channel: own-position (M1/M5), feedback coordination (M2) and state coordination (R0).
- **Experiment C (content):** replace a neighbour's feedback with the feedback from a closely matched prompt that steers toward the other answer, and test whether the target follows. Controls are feedback of matched uncertainty and random perturbations.
- **Experiment D (significance):** cut the cross-position feedback channel everywhere while keeping each position's own feedback, and measure how much of SC's coherence and task benefit is lost.
- **Experiment E (dynamics):** whether SC makes positions commit jointly, and whether removing it delays commitment at later positions.
- **Instrument checks:**
  - a positive control with a planted signal, to confirm the interventions detect a known channel;
  - out-of-distribution checks on every mixed on/off intervention (the model was never trained with feedback on at some positions and off at others).

### 6.4 Decision rules
- Each confirmatory estimate is compared with a pre-registered minimum effect of interest, using a 95% interval clustered by prompt.
  - An estimate is **supported** if its lower bound exceeds the minimum.
  - It is **rejected** if its upper bound falls below the minimum.
  - Otherwise it is **inconclusive**, and an inconclusive result is never treated as a negative one.
- The primary family is the direct effect beyond the state, the coordination share, P1 and P2, with Holm correction.
- Every combination of outcomes maps to a pre-specified interpretation and paper framing.

## 7. Expected Contributions
1. **The first measurement of mode mixing in a real continuous flow LM**, with the model's own AR head as a within-model reference. The finding holds whichever mechanism wins.
2. **A characterization of what self-conditioning fixes:** within-block coherence at later positions, not per-token accuracy. It is independent of guidance strength.
3. **A causal decomposition of the self-conditioning benefit** into own-position, state-coordination and feedback-coordination channels, and a finding about how quickly the latent state absorbs the feedback at deployed settings.
4. **If M2 holds:** evidence that self-conditioning is how parallel positions agree on one continuation.

**Planned framing:** lead with the phenomenon (1–2), then the mechanism (3–4).

### 8.3 Main risks
- **Hybrids may largely disappear at 7B.** This would make P1 a small-model effect; a scale check is scheduled early.
- **The mixed on/off interventions may be too far out of distribution.** The fallback is designs that swap and erase signals rather than switching feedback off.
- **The feedback channel may matter mainly in the training-noise regime.** In that case the deployed benefit would be attributed mostly to own-position or state channels; this is reported either way.
- **Engineering scope.** Lower-priority analyses are designated to be cut first.
- **Being scooped** by groups active in this area.

### 8.4 Current outlook (subjective)
- About 70% chance of a solid paper built on the phenomenon and decomposition.
- About 20% chance that feedback coordination is confirmed as a headline mechanism.
